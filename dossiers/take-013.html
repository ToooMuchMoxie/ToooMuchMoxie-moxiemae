Moxie Mae’s Take #013 — Dossier

Cite Your Source

Angela Rye's reparations panel said the quiet parts out loud — and twice, Rye had to tell her own panelist: cite your source.

The Claim

On “The Truth About Reparations in America” (Cite Your Source with Angela Rye, Oct 15, 2026), the panel split on the question that decides whether a reparations claim survives court: is eligibility a racial claim, or a claim by a harmed group — the people enslaved and emancipated in the U.S.? Devine Prince of the U.S. Freedmen Project argued lineage; the pushback is on tape.

The Receipts

Receipt #1 — Confirmed — America has paid for slavery before

The D.C. Compensated Emancipation Act of 1862 paid ENSLAVERS up to $300 per person freed — 930 petitions covering 2,900+ people, the largest payout $18,000 for 69 people. Rye's close, verbatim: “Let no one tell you this country doesn't know how to pay for slavery. It did pay. It paid the wrong people.”

Source: Panel + historical record (Take #013 verified receipts)

Receipt #2 — Confirmed — the world called it

UN General Assembly Resolution A/RES/80/250 (Mar 25, 2026) declared the transatlantic slave trade “the gravest crime against humanity,” 123–3. The NO votes: the U.S., Israel, Argentina — the U.S. objecting in part because the resolution affirms reparations.

Source: UN / ISHR / People (Take #013 verified receipts)

Receipt #3 — Confirmed — the delivery receipt

Amazon's $8.25M D.C. settlement: $7.25M in refunds to ~69,000 Prime members in ZIPs 20019/20020 (Wards 7 & 8) plus a $1M penalty, after Amazon excluded those ZIPs from its own delivery network from 2022 while charging full Prime — two-day delivery rates fell ~72% to ~25%. No admission of fault.

Source: D.C. AG Schwalb announcement; Black Enterprise; FOX 5 DC (Take #013 verified receipts)

Receipt #4 — Verbatim — the Freedmen exchange

Prince: “The power of Freedmen is, when you say Freedmen you don't have to say Black, because this country only enslaved Black people. But when you say Black, now you're including people that was never enslaved in this country at all.” Tigani: “I think being focused on documentation is problematic.” Rye, twice, to Prince: “I'm gonna need you to cite your source.”

Source: Panel transcript, whisper-checked against the source video (Take #013 file; source video on file)

Mae’s Verdict“It did pay. It paid the wrong people.” A claim built on lineage and documentation is a claim built to survive the Equal Protection Clause — Prince's argument, on the record, is that the clause “is a sword that swings in both directions.”
