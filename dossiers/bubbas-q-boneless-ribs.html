Bubba’s Q: The Patent Was Real. So Was Everything After.

A clip going around LinkedIn and Instagram shows Al “Bubba” Baker — former NFL defensive end — stunning the Shark Tank panel with something Kevin O’Leary said he’d never seen on the show: a patent on a food product. Mae pulled the patent records, the episode record, the fast-food deal, and the federal court file. The clip is the first act. Here is the whole play.

Status — Research DossierThis is a research dossier, compiled November 16, 2026 — it is not a published Moxie Mae Take. Whether it becomes one is Tooo Much’s call, and it’s pending. Every claim below carries the label the research gave it: CONFIRMED (primary source), SOURCED (reputable reporting), or UNPROVEN (claimed, never verified).

The Man

James Albert London Baker — the name on the patent itself (CONFIRMED as to that form). Defensive end out of Colorado State, drafted by Detroit in 1978 (2nd round, 40th overall), 13 NFL seasons: Lions, Cardinals, Browns, Vikings (CONFIRMED/SOURCED, Pro Football Reference and the Detroit Lions). NFL Defensive Rookie of the Year, 1978; First-team All-Pro; 3× Pro Bowl (SOURCED). His 23 rookie sacks are unofficial — sacks weren’t an official stat until 1982.

After football: barbecue. Bubba’s Q in Avon, Ohio — opened ~2006, ran 13 years, closed October 2019 (SOURCED, Cleveland Scene; Detroit Lions). The de-boning idea started at home: his wife Sabrina found ribs too messy, so Baker worked out how to take the bones out and leave the meat whole, to be eaten with a knife and fork. His daughter Brittani Bo Baker — spelled Brittani — is co-inventor on both patents (CONFIRMED, the patents name her) and was the restaurant’s managing partner by her own account.

The Patents — Checked Against the Record

There are exactly two Baker rib patents (CONFIRMED, USPTO/Google Patents). Write these numbers down, because the internet keeps getting them wrong:

Receipt #1 — The viral claim — CONFIRMED in substance

“A patent for the product AND a patent for the process” is substantially accurate: Patent 1 holds genuine product claims (and process claims), and Patent 2 is a dedicated process patent. The lawyer’s caveat: Patent 1 is not a pure product patent — it bundles product, process and packaged-product claims in one document. And the method is exactly as pitched: cook with the bones in, then remove them with the meat intact — not a ground, reshaped patty.

Source: USPTO patent PDFs; Google Patents

Receipt #2 — The wrong numbers going around

A widely shared patent-firm analysis cites “U.S. Patent No. 7,959,595” — wrong; the process patent is 7,959,500. Content-farm blogs cite “8,206,205” — not a Baker rib patent in the records checked. If the number isn’t 7,666,075 or 7,959,500, it isn’t his rib patent.

Source: Bold Patents analysis (number error); USPTO records

The Pitch — December 6, 2013

Receipt #3 — The episode — SOURCED

Shark Tank Season 5, Episode 11, ABC, December 6, 2013. Panel: Mark Cuban, Daymond John, Kevin O’Leary, Barbara Corcoran, Robert Herjavec. (Viral summaries that put Lori Greiner on this panel are wrong.) The ask: $300,000 for 15%. Baker stated $154,000 in sales on air, through ~48 Ohio stores.

Source: ABC episode listings; contemporary Cleveland reporting; SUCCESS/CNBC

Receipt #4 — The moment — SOURCED (transcript)

Baker, holding up two documents: “Right here, Kevin, is the patent for the product. And right here is the patent for the process.” On the method: “If I tell you, I’d have to kill you” — and 20 years of know-how beyond the patent. O’Leary, in substance: in the entire history of the show, he had never seen a patent on a food product. That was a Shark marvelling, not a legal opinion.

Source: Clip transcript, corroborated across dozens of identical reposts

Receipt #5 — The deal — SOURCED

O’Leary offered $300,000 for 49%, contingent on licensing the patent to a major meat processor. Baker accepted Daymond John’s $300,000 for 30% — double the equity he asked for — with licensing to a major pork producer part of the agreement. Whether a signed contract on those exact terms ever closed is disputed; see The Fall.

Source: Shark Tank recaps; Cleveland Scene, Dec 2013

The Rise

  • After the episode (SOURCED): $150,000 in online orders in 24 hours, over $400,000 by the end of the first week (SUCCESS, May 2016, quoting Baker). The LA Times later reported ~$250,000 in launch orders with ~14% refunded.
  • The manufacturer (CONFIRMED as a fact of the venture, court opinions): from 2015, Rastelli Foods Group of New Jersey became manufacturer/co-packer and partner in the joint venture, FOF Bakers LLC.
  • The fast-food deal (CONFIRMED, CKE’s own release): on April 13–14, 2017, Carl’s Jr. and Hardee’s announced the Baby Back Rib Burger — ribs “officially and exclusively supplied by Bubba’s-Q” — on sale April 26, 2017 at $5.59 (double $6.79). CNBC reported the ribs in ~3,000 restaurants with 1 million pounds ordered (SOURCED — the ~3,000 figure is reporting, not CKE’s release).

Receipt #6 — “$16 million in sales” — SOURCED as a TV claim, UNPROVEN as an audited fact

The number comes from the April 14, 2017 Shark Tank update segment, reported by CNBC the next day: Baker on camera — “It’s been three years and sales are at $16 million.” No audit, filing, or financial statement supporting it was found in any source. It is a sales figure announced on entertainment TV — routinely mis-copied downstream as “profits” or “net worth.” The closest paper figure: the 2019 settlement acknowledged $14.5 million in gross receipts (LA Times, which viewed the agreement). Gross receipts are not profit either.

Source: CNBC, Apr 15, 2017; LA Times, May 18, 2023

The Fall — Two Accounts, and What the Courts Actually Did

Read this the way Mae reads everything: two competing accounts, then the part a judge signed. The courts decided contract and disparagement questions. They never adjudicated the underlying accounting the Bakers complain about.

Receipt #7 — The Bakers’ account — SOURCED as their claim, UNPROVEN as accounting

Per the LA Times (Stacy Perman, May 18, 2023), the family says: the on-air deal was revised off-air to $100,000 for 35% (no final contract has ever been made public); they were projected to make ~$193,000 from the fast-food deal and instead received ~$2,900 profit plus a $61,917.45 licensing fee; their total take was $659,653 — about 4% of the publicised $16 million. They also blame Nate Holzapfel — a fellow Shark Tank contestant John connected them with to run e-commerce — for a sales-tax failure that forced >$30,000 in refunds the day after airing, and say he closed a ~$100,000 account in June 2014 and sent them an $8,000 cheque. Holzapfel was never charged over Baker money; his later Utah guilty pleas (2023, communications fraud and sexual battery) were in unrelated cases — do not conflate them. Every figure in this receipt is family-supplied and was never tested in court.

Source: LA Times investigation, May 18, 2023

Receipt #8 — John’s account — CONFIRMED where a court adopted it

John says he was a non-managing partner whose role was brand ambassador — the face of the brand, connections, social media — with no access to the company’s books or accounts. A federal judge’s 2023 findings of fact say the same, and add: John invested, at minimum, two $100,000 contributions and received only ~$112,000–$113,000 back in distributions. After the ruling, John: “The actual facts, the record, and the federal judge’s opinion have confirmed that I did not — and could not have — committed any wrongdoing.”

Source: DF Ventures v. FOFBakers, Doc. 47 (Kugler, J., Jul 21, 2023); John statement via People/AfroTech

Receipt #9 — What the courts did — CONFIRMED (opinions read)

2019: an earlier breakdown ended in a settlement with mutual confidentiality and non-disparagement terms, a larger gross-profit share for the Bakers, and $100,000 from Rastelli in 12 instalments. July 21, 2023: Judge Kugler found the Bakers’ LA Times and social-media statements breached that settlement, made the injunction against them permanent, and ordered the disparaging posts down; in the parallel case he found Rastelli in full compliance — monthly financial reports provided — and noted the Bakers never exercised their audit rights. March 5, 2024: $316,900.46 in attorneys’ fees and costs awarded to John’s/Rastelli’s side. March 31, 2025: the Bakers’ motions for a receiver and forensic accountant denied — already decided. Note what the injunction is and isn’t: it is a finding that publishing the claims breached the deal they signed — not a finding that every accounting claim was false.

Source: Federal court opinions, D.N.J., 2023–2025 (CourtListener/Justia/govinfo)

Receipt #10 — The 2026 ruling — CONFIRMED, and read the fine print

The Bakers’ own 2024 suit alleged Rastelli infringed the patents by selling “Rastelli’s Boneless Ribs” on QVC from April 2024 with no royalties paid 2020–June 2023. On March 26, 2026, Judge Kiel dismissed it for lack of standing: the patents belong to JabezBaker LLC, not to the Bakers personally — and the LLC had been dismissed because it appeared without a lawyer. The opinion states in terms: “This decision does not reach the merits of plaintiffs’ claims.” Whether the ribs infringe was never decided.

Source: Baker v. Rastelli Foods, 1:24-cv-08882, Doc. 30 (Kiel, J., Mar 26, 2026)

Where It Stands Now

  • Bubba’s Q is not selling ribs under that brand today (verified live, November 16, 2026: bubbasbonelessribs.com returns 403, no storefront). Secondary reporting says the company announced it ceased production in May 2023 — “what began as an exciting opportunity on Shark Tank quickly turned into a nightmare” — with online sales winding down during 2023 (SOURCED; no surviving primary announcement).
  • The product lives on under the other side’s name: Rastelli’s Boneless Ribs are sold via QVC and Rastelli’s (reviewed by The Kitchn, Sept 2024) — the very product the 2024 suit complained about (SOURCED).
  • The patents belong to JabezBaker LLC, the Baker-family company (CONFIRMED, 2026 opinion + USPTO records) — not “JABZ Baker,” and not to Al and Brittani personally. Google Patents listed the process patent’s anticipated expiration as November 9, 2026; what that means for enforceability is a question for counsel, not a conclusion this file draws.
  • The family today (SOURCED): the 2026 opinion lists Al as a Florida citizen and Brittani as a California citizen. Brittani’s current public project is a proposal for a Santa Monica BBQ beach café — whether it was awarded or opened is UNPROVEN.

What the Viral Clip Leaves Out

The clip shows the patent reveal, O’Leary’s amazement, the “kill you” joke, and the $154,000. It leaves out: that the accepted deal was 30%, not 15%; the family’s claim it changed again off-air; the entire FOF Bakers/Rastelli structure that decided who got paid; that “$16 million” was a TV-announced sales figure, never audited; the 2019 restaurant closure and settlement; the permanent injunction and the $316,900.46 fee award; the 2026 dismissal; and the fact that Bubba’s Q ribs aren’t sold anymore while Rastelli’s sells a boneless rib on QVC. One honest gap in this file: the original LinkedIn post itself isn’t indexed and couldn’t be read signed-out — the clip was identified through dozens of identical 2025–26 reposts whose transcripts match word-for-word.

Could Not Verify

  • The final signed deal terms — never made public by either side.
  • All dispute accounting ($193K projection, $2,900, $61,917.45, $659,653, the Holzapfel money) — family-supplied, never adjudicated.
  • Any audit of the “$16 million” — none found.
