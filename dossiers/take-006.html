Moxie Mae’s Take #006 — Dossier

I'm Not Black, I'm Ethiopian

An Ethiopian creator went viral saying she's “not Black.” Her own community checked her — and she apologized. Mae amplifies the check, not a pile-on.

The Claim

The argument was that “Black” is a slavery-and-colonization label you can opt out of if your country was never colonized. Ethiopia's unconquered history is real — Adwa, 1896. The error is using it to exit Blackness.

The Receipts

Receipt #1 — The word itself

“Ethiopia” comes from the Greek Aithiopia — aithō (“I burn”) + ōps (“face”): “burnt-face.” For Greek and Roman writers it denoted the dark-skinned peoples south of Egypt; Ptolemy used “Aethiopia” as the general name for sub-Saharan Africa's peoples, “black in complexion.” The country's name is the ancient world's word for Black people.

Source: Take #006 verified facts (researched 2026-10-12): Greek etymology, Ptolemy, Herodotus

Receipt #2 — 1808, New York

Ethiopian sailors and free Black Americans walked out of a segregated church together and founded the Abyssinian Baptist Church — “Abyssinian” means Ethiopian. In 1954, Emperor Haile Selassie presented the church a sacred Coptic cross. Black Americans named one of their most historic churches after Ethiopia.

Source: Take #006 verified facts

Receipt #3 — Her own community

She was checked by Black Americans AND by Ethiopians, immigrants and locals — and issued a public apology. The Take names no name and piles on no person: the smoke is for the argument.

Source: Coverage compiled in the Take #006 file (African Diaspora News Channel live; apology coverage)
