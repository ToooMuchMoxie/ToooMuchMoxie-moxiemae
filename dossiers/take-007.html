Moxie Mae’s Take #007 — Dossier

$1.5 Million and Mad About Parking

A couple paid $1.5 million for a house across from Huston-Tillotson University — then fought HBCU students over public street parking.

The Claim

On camera, the homeowner said “We paid $1.5 million for our house” and “This is OUR NEIGHBORHOOD,” while students documented notes left on their cars and a professor's car blocked in with trash bins. Buying a house buys no rights to the street.

The Receipts

Receipt #1 — The law

City of Austin Transportation & Public Works, on the record: “Parking is allowed on those blocks of East 9th Street… the curb is otherwise open for public parking.” Texas Transportation Code §545.302 lists where you may NOT park — a public curb in front of a house is not on the list.

Source: City of Austin via FOX 7 Austin; Texas Transportation Code §545.302 (Take #007 verified facts)

Receipt #2 — The history

Huston-Tillotson was founded in 1875 — one of the oldest HBCUs west of the Mississippi. East Austin's Black community was shaped by the city's 1928 master plan that pushed Black residents east of I-35. The school and the neighborhood predate the mortgage by about 150 years.

Source: Take #007 verified facts (researched 2026-10-13)

Receipt #3 — The neighbors

16-year resident Sybill Bradley said student parking “has long been the norm.” Students answered with a sign: “You bought a house, not our history. East Austin is ours.”

Source: FOX 7 Austin and Atlanta Black Star coverage, compiled in the Take #007 file

Mae’s Verdict“You bought a house, ma'am, not the street in front of it — and not our history.” Honesty note from the file: vandalized vehicles could NOT be verified in coverage, so the Take stayed on the documented record — notes on cars and the bin blockade.
