Javits Frimpong: Y’all Named a Killer Before the Police Did

Javits Badu Nkansah Frimpong was 15 years old, a Ghanaian kid one year into his new life in the Bronx, shot on his walk to school on October 29, 2026. For thirteen days, police identified no suspect and said nothing about race — and the internet filled the silence with a story: that Black American school bullies killed the African kid. Mae pulled the case record. Here is what it actually says, what it doesn’t say, and who said the rest.

Status — Research DossierThis is a research dossier, compiled November 16, 2026 — it is not a published Moxie Mae Take. A script draft drawn from this file is awaiting Tooo Much’s approval; no video has been built. The teen charged is charged, not convicted, and is presumed innocent. Labels: CONFIRMED (primary/official source), SOURCED (reputable reporting), UNPROVEN (claimed online, not verified).

The Bottom Line

No official source — not the NYPD, not the Bronx DA, not any court record or reputable news report — identifies the suspect as African American or Black American. And no official source confirms he is Guinean, Fulani, Senegalese, Sierra Leonean, or Ghanaian either. Those labels are inferences from a name, made by commentators, and they contradict each other.

Who Javits Was

Javits Badu Nkansah Frimpong, 15 (CONFIRMED — Ghana’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs statement, October 30, 2026; the spelling variants in social posts are errors). He arrived from Ghana on October 28, 2025 to live with his father, Thomas Frimpong, 60, a New York City cab driver, in Claremont, the Bronx — he was killed one year and one day after arriving (SOURCED). His mother, Doris Adu Gyamfi, lives in Kumasi, Ghana; Javits was her only child. His sister Millicent is a U.S. Army veteran. He was a 10th grader at DreamYard Preparatory School, described as quiet and studious, dreaming of becoming a physician and serving in the military first — his sister read his journal at the family’s press conference: “I am from Ghana… School has been good since I came to the United States.” NYPD Chief of Detectives Joseph Kenny: “just a very good kid” — no indication of any gang involvement.

What Happened — October 29, 2026

Receipt #1 — The shooting — CONFIRMED/SOURCED

Thursday, October 29, 2026, ~9:24 a.m., East 171st Street at Crotona Place, Claremont, near Crotona Park (the same corner is also reported as East 171st & Third Avenue — one incident, two reportings). Surveillance video shows three hooded teens in black and blue surgical masks, who had been waiting in the park, chase him. He dropped his backpack and ran about a block before collapsing. 42nd Precinct officers drove him to St. Barnabas Hospital, where he died the same day.

Source: NYPD via PEOPLE and ABC7; Ghana MFA statement

Receipt #2 — The details police gave — SOURCED

Chief Kenny’s November 2 briefing: the shooters went back and collected the spent shell casings; a bystander took the dropped backpack, never recovered; the three fled through the Freeman Street station on a southbound No. 5 train. Police said they were weighing three theories — bullying, robbery gone bad, mistaken identity as a rival gang member — and had subpoenaed the Department of Education for school records. One discrepancy the file will not paper over: early reports say he was shot once in the abdomen/stomach; Kenny later said “shot him in the back.” Outlets have not reconciled the two.

Source: NYPD Chief of Detectives briefing, Nov 2, 2026 (ABC7); NY Post

Receipt #3 — The father’s account — SOURCED as his account, not a police finding

Thomas Frimpong said the principal called about two days before the shooting: two boys who had fought with Javits were suspended for a week; Javits had complained of being bullied at lunch. Police never confirmed those students were suspects — and later said they were not the shooters (Receipt #6). This account, honestly reported by his grieving father, is what the online story was built from.

Source: FOX 5 New York, Oct 29, 2026; NY Post

The Arrest

Receipt #4 — One arrest, November 11 — CONFIRMED

On Wednesday morning, November 11, 2026, the NYPD arrested a 17-year-old at his Bronx home (Violent Crimes Task Force, 42nd Precinct) and charged him with second-degree murder, manslaughter, and criminal possession of a weapon. He was arraigned that evening in Bronx Criminal Court and held without bail. Per the criminal complaint as reported by Gothamist, someone who knows him identified him in surveillance footage, and ballistics and witness information tied him and two others to the shooting. Two other suspects are still sought. Police have not said whether he fired the shot, and have not established a motive. A Bronx grand jury was scheduled to consider indictment on Monday, November 16 — no outcome had been reported as of this research.

Source: NYPD announcement via AP and PEOPLE, Nov 11, 2026; Gothamist, Nov 12, 2026

The Name — And What It Does and Does Not Prove

Receipt #5 — The name — SOURCED, with its provenance on the label

The NYPD has never officially released the suspect’s name — he is a minor, and most major outlets withhold it. The name Thierno Jalloh comes from law-enforcement sources speaking to the New York Post, and from the name being stated on the record in open court, as PIX11’s reporter said on air outside Bronx Criminal Court; prosecutors used the name in Gothamist’s and amNY’s reporting. That is the provenance. Print it with the name, every time.

Source: NY Post, Nov 11, 2026; PIX11 (Eileen Lehpamer), Nov 11–12; Gothamist, Nov 12; amNY, Nov 13

Receipt #6 — The ethnicity question — the critical receipt

No NYPD release, Bronx DA release, court report, or reputable news report found states the suspect’s race, ethnicity, nationality, birthplace, or immigration status. AmNewsWorld (Nov 14) says it flatly: police have not confirmed his nationality or ethnic background, and claims identifying him as an immigrant from Guinea and of Fulani descent have not been independently established. Was he identified as African American / Black American by any official or reputable source? No. Is he confirmed Guinean/Fulani/African? Also no — that is an inference from the name, and the online versions contradict each other: Guinean, Senegalese, Sierra Leonean, even Ghanaian, all claimed, none documented. Mutually contradictory claims cannot all be receipts.

Source: AmNewsWorld, Nov 14, 2026; research across NYPD/DA/court record and NYC outlets

Receipt #7 — What IS documented about the suspect — SOURCED

Two facts: he did not attend Javits’s school (law-enforcement sources, NY Post), and on November 12–13 the family’s own attorney, Ben Crump, said prosecutors told the family the shooters were not the bullying-incident students and they did not believe bullying was the motive (Gothamist; amNY). That is enough to say the “Black American school bully killed him” story is unsupported and contradicted on its school/bullying element. It is not enough to declare the suspect’s ethnicity as fact — so this file doesn’t.

Source: NY Post, Nov 11, 2026; Gothamist, Nov 12; amNY, Nov 13

The Claim That Ran Ahead of the Record

The chain, documented link by link: (1) a grieving father reports bullying and suspensions; (2) for thirteen days police identify no one and describe only three masked teens — saying nothing about race, ethnicity, or nationality; (3) online, the suspended students were assumed to be Black American, and the killing was framed as Black American violence against an African immigrant. Step 3 was never supported by any police identification.

  • The pushback started before any arrest (SOURCED). DahTruth, “Crying Wolf in the Bronx,” November 1: “According to them, it was American Black boys, and it was American Blacks as a people who shot him in the back. No suspect has been named, no arrest has been made, no motive has been established…” Charlie Braxton on Medium: Ghanaians “automatically assume that the culprit was African American.” Cardi B, on Instagram Live, pushed back on the framing directly: “Everybody… be bullying the new kids that come from foreign countries… even their own kind.”
  • After the arrest, the corrections (SOURCED). Tariq Nasheed on X, answering Crump’s diaspora-unity post: “Now that it’s been revealed that one of the suspects is African, now people wanna talk about ‘everybody should be united.’ Hell no” — quoted here as his words; the “revealed African” premise is the name-inference, UNPROVEN as ethnicity. Anthony Brian Logan (Nov 12) and Tim Black (Nov 13) made the fuller correction case; Instagram and Facebook correction reels ran November 12–13.
  • No single originator could be verified (UNPROVEN). The claim spread as an assumption attached to the bullying story across X, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook and YouTube — not as one traceable viral post — and no formal fact-check of the ethnicity claim was found. Any video naming one creator as the originator needs a primary link this research did not find.
Mae’s VerdictThe verdict this file supports — and the only one it supports: the claim that a Black American killed Javits Frimpong is UNPROVEN and, in its school-bully form, contradicted by the documented record. The counter-claim that the killer is definitively a Guinean, Fulani, or African immigrant is also UNPROVEN — it rests on a name. Two suspects remain unidentified, so no group claim about “the killers” can be sourced at all. A child is dead. His mother buried her only son. The receipts say: name the standard, then hold everybody to it — including us.

The Family and the Community

  • Ghana’s government (CONFIRMED, Oct 30): Ministry of Foreign Affairs statement with President Mahama’s condolences; Foreign Minister Ablakwa to the father: “We will pursue justice.” Ghana’s missions visited the family and pledged legal and consular help.
  • The mother’s journey (SOURCED): Mayor Mamdani said he phoned President Trump (Oct 31), who agreed to an emergency visa. Doris Adu Gyamfi arrived in New York November 4. At the November 11 press conference at the National Action Network in Harlem, beside Crump and Sharpton: “I need justice for my child. I have only one child” — then she collapsed. The father: “I need justice for my son.” An uncle, to the Post: “One in the bag, two to go.”
  • Vigils and marches (SOURCED): October 31 at the collapse site; DreamYard students walked out November 2 chanting his name; a one-week vigil November 5 with hundreds and white balloons; a march from Lou Gehrig Plaza to East 171st Street November 7.
  • GoFundMe (SOURCED, dated snapshots — do not conflate): “Support for Javits Frimpong’s Family,” organized by his cousin Tracy Boampong: $254,119 by October 30; $898,199 from 27,000+ donations by November 5; $911,335 by November 9. The family warns only this campaign is legitimate.
  • Funeral (SOURCED): scheduled Friday, November 20, 2026, 9 a.m.–1 p.m., Boston Road, the Bronx (one outlet gives 1175 Boston Road; another gives 175 — flagged, not merged), burial at Kensico Cemetery.
