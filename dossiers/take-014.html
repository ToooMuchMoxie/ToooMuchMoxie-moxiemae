7.0% Is Not Our Number

The September jobs report put Black unemployment at 7.0%. Mae reads the fine print — including the part where the data has no line for us at all.

The Claim

The Bureau of Labor Statistics' September report (released Oct 2, 2026) put Black unemployment at 7.0%, up from 6.0% in August, while the overall rate was 4.2% and every other tracked group went DOWN. But BLS counts by race, not by lineage: there is no FBA unemployment rate in this data, and Mae won't pretend a broad category answers a specific question.

The Receipts

Receipt #1 — The number

Black unemployment 7.0% (from 6.0% in August); overall 4.2%; white 3.6%, Hispanic 4.7%, Asian 2.9% — and the only number that climbed was ours. Black women took the sharpest hit, 5.9% to 7.3%. The economy added just 29,000 jobs, with the prior two months revised DOWN by 60,000.

Source: Bureau of Labor Statistics, September jobs report (Take #014 script, Seg 1)

Receipt #2 — Read it honest

One month is a warning light, not a verdict: this same rate was 8.2% last November and had been falling all year, and part of September's jump is more people stepping INTO the labor force. And there is NO line in the report for Foundational Black Americans — no lineage data exists in it.

Source: Take #014 script, Seg 2 (“the standards/honesty segment”)

Receipt #3 — The builders

Brookings, from Census Bureau business data: in 2023 Black-owned EMPLOYER businesses passed 200,000 for the first time ever — up 62% since 2017 while employer businesses overall grew 3% — with $249 billion in revenue, 1.8 million jobs, and Black women employers up 78%. Same honesty rule: that's the Census race category, 2023 data — background, not this morning's news. The gap: 14.4% of the population, 3.4% of employer owners.

Source: Brookings Institution analysis of Census Bureau data (Take #014 script, Seg 3)

Mae’s Verdict“7.0% is not our number” — it's a warning light on a dashboard that doesn't even have our name on it. The Take lands on the standard that makes a Black business able to hire and KEEP somebody: customers who come back, a price that can carry the labor, clean records, and delivering what you promised, every time.
