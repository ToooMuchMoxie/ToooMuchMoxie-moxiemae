Moxie Mae’s Take #001 — Dossier

The Diaspora Wars

Why Black Americans are a distinct ethnic group — and why saying so is history, not hate.

The Claim

Black Americans are a distinct ethnic group, forged in American slavery, Jim Crow, and the civil rights movement — a specific lineage with a specific claim. Love for Caribbean and African brothers and sisters doesn't require flattening that into one big “Black.”

The Receipts

Receipt #1 — The door opener

The Civil Rights Act of 1964 is what forced America to open its doors — then the Immigration Act of 1965 let the rest of the diaspora walk through them. The movement Black Americans built changed who could come to America.

Source: Take #001 script, receipt card #1

Receipt #2 — Delineation is already the law's language

When it's time to talk reparations, lineage suddenly matters: California's Reparations Task Force had to delineate — American Descendants of Slavery, specifically.

Source: Take #001 script, receipt card #2 (California Reparations Task Force)

Mae’s Verdict“Respect the delineation. We can build together — but don't flatten us into one big 'Black' and then act surprised when we say: nah. We're FBA. And that's specific.”
