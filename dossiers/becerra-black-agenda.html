Flagship Dossier — October 2026

Asked Point Blank: Xavier Becerra’s “Black Agenda” Answer

Xavier Becerra — former U.S. Health & Human Services Secretary and California Attorney General — is running for Governor of California in the November 3, 2026 general election. On Tavis Smiley’s show on KBLA Talk 1580 (Los Angeles’s Black-owned talk station), he was asked directly what his Black agenda for California is. Mae pulled the tape, the transcript, his written plan, and his record. Here is all of it.

The Moment — Watch It Yourself

Full show stream, KBLA 1580’s YouTube channel. Jump straight to the receipts: The question — 2:27:28 · His answer — 2:27:45 · Episode audio + full transcript: iHeart — “Sec. Xavier Becerra joins Tavis Smiley” (episode dated October 16, 2026).

The Question — Verbatim

“Let me ask you, I got less than five minutes here, more like three or four. Let me squeeze through this right quick. Your black agenda. I’d be remiss if I didn’t ask you point blank. You’re asking for the votes of black Californians. What are you going to do for them?” — Tavis Smiley

The Answer — Verbatim

“And Tavis, I want every black voter in California to look at me and say, we got to vote for this guy because he’s going to give us an opportunity. What I’m not going to do is I’m not going to promise you 40 acres and a mule because I know what happens when you make those kinds of promises. I want to deliver. When I worked on health care, I wanted to deliver results, and we did. When I was AG, I wanted to deliver results. That’s why I took on this issue of cash bail, because I saw as attorney general that there were people who had not yet been convicted of a crime who were sitting in a jail. Why? Because they didn’t have the money to bail themselves out. And so that’s not fair. That’s not right. We’re going to fight for true opportunity, true justice, and I hope that the black community sees that they’ve got somebody who will champion that for them.” — Xavier Becerra

Read it again and count what is not in it: no reparations. No homeownership. No schools. No Black business. The only concrete policy example in the whole answer is cash bail from his Attorney General years. (Wording corroborated by WorldNetDaily, the California Globe, and Inland Valley News coverage of the same interview.)

Receipt Two — He Has a Plan. On Paper.

Becerra’s Black-specific commitments live almost entirely in one document: his written answers to the Black Action Alliance 2026 gubernatorial questionnaire (published verbatim as submitted). In it he commits to:

Receipt #1 — Homeownership for descendants

Dedicated funding for down-payment assistance and anti-displacement for verified descendants of American slavery, through a Bureau for Descendants of American Slavery — “The target is 10,000 families into homeownership with substantial equity stakes.” Plus non-dilutive grants to Black-owned businesses.

Source: Becerra’s BAA questionnaire, Question 8 (verbatim)

Receipt #2 — Black renters & Black babies

“My first intervention is a Homelessness Prevention Fund for Black Renters” — emergency rental help and legal representation in high-displacement neighborhoods. And “PART A: YES” to permanent state funding for Black Infant Health, with measurable reductions in Black maternal mortality and expanded Medi-Cal doula access.

Source: BAA questionnaire, Questions 12 and 5 (verbatim)

Receipt #3 — Business & procurement

A California Community Reinvestment Fund for Black, Indigenous, and historically excluded entrepreneurs — and a commitment to meet and exceed the state’s 25% small-business / 5% microbusiness procurement goals.

Source: BAA questionnaire, Question 7 (verbatim)

Receipt #4 — Testing Prop 209

“Yes, I support legislation that tests the boundaries of Proposition 209 where there is a reasonable legal pathway… descendant-based eligibility, and place-based investments in historically harmed communities all remain viable.”

Source: BAA questionnaire, Question 8, Part A (verbatim)

His public campaign website tells a different story: its priorities are general affordability, housing and health planks for everyone. There is no Black-specific page on xavierbecerra2026.com. The plan exists — in a questionnaire PDF, not on the stump, and not in his answer to Tavis Smiley.

Receipt Three — The Missing Number

Receipt #5 — No dollar figure — anywhere

Nowhere — not in the questionnaire, not at the January 2026 Urban League forum (where, pressed on reparations, he said he was in favor but asked “What comes first? And how do you fund it?”), and not in the Smiley interview — has Becerra committed to cash reparations or to any dollar figure for the Bureau for Descendants of American Slavery.

Source: BAA Executive Summary, May 27, 2026; San Francisco Chronicle via Fox News (Jan 2026 forum)

“No candidate has committed to a specific dollar figure for the Bureau for Descendants of American Slavery. This is the single most consequential omission across the field.” — Black Action Alliance Executive Summary, May 27, 2026

Fair is fair, and Mae prints the whole receipt: the same Black Action Alliance still rates Becerra Tier A (3.5) and the field’s only “Strong” on reparations — based on the questionnaire’s specificity and his AG record. The omission they flag belongs to the whole field. He is, on paper, the strongest of a field that won’t name a number.

The Record — What He’s Done

  • Cash bail (AG): His office reversed position and backed the Humphrey ruling — it “will not defend any application of the bail law that does not take into consideration a person’s ability to pay” — and declined to appeal, making ability-to-pay the statewide rule.
  • Civil rights (AG): The 2019 Sausalito Marin City desegregation action — described as the first in California in 50 years; the Stockton Unified schools-policing investigation and settlement; 120+ lawsuits against the first Trump administration.
  • Stephon Clark (AG) — the hard receipt: On March 5, 2019, Becerra announced no criminal charges against the two Sacramento officers who shot Stephon Clark, an unarmed 22-year-old holding a cellphone. NPR reported “his words gave no comfort to Stephon Clark’s family,” and BLM Sacramento’s Tanya Faison attacked the use-of-force standard the decision rested on.
  • Health (HHS Secretary): 12-month postpartum Medicaid — HHS’s ASPE estimated roughly 130,000 Black beneficiaries a year at full adoption — and major investment in community health centers. The BAA’s honest summary of his HHS tenure: “low-visibility, cautious, and reactive rather than transformational.”
  • Endorsements: Kamala Harris, Jim Clyburn, Jasmine Crockett, Tony Thurmond and Malia Cohen are among the Black officials backing him.

The Reaction

The October answer drew fire within days — from Republican opponent Steve Hilton (Oct. 19 statement: “Instead of talking about expanding homeownership, improving schools, or lowering the cost of living, Becerra’s first concrete example was bail reform”), Oakland activist Seneca Scott, the California Globe (“tone-deaf”), and commentator Kira Davis: “His big plan to help black Californians? Zero cash bail.” Former Reparations Task Force member Lisa Holder told KQED that advocates are still searching for a reparations champion in this race.
